# Client communication

Clients want to know: what happened, what it means for them, what they need to decide,
and what happens next. Write for a busy, intelligent non-lawyer.

Plugin root is two levels above this skill's directory. Follow
`<plugin>/references/ground-rules.md`.

## Structure (advice letter / update email)
1. **Bottom line first** - one or two sentences: the answer or the news.
2. **What it means for you** - consequences in money, time, risk, options.
3. **Options and our recommendation** - each option with pros, cons, cost and time;
   make a clear recommendation and say why.
4. **What we need from you** - decisions, documents, information, with dates.
5. **Next steps and timeline** - who does what by when; next hearing / deadline.
6. **Key caveats** - assumptions and the main risk, briefly. No wall of disclaimers.

## Style
- Short sentences, active voice, everyday words. Explain any unavoidable legal term once.
- Give numbers and ranges rather than "significant" or "substantial". If uncertain, say
  so and give the likelihood in words ("more likely than not", "a real risk") consistently.
- Don't overstate prospects; never promise outcomes.
- Mark privileged communications: "Privileged & Confidential - Legal Advice".
- Match the client's language preference; for Indian clients, offer a Hindi or regional-language
  version if helpful (confirm legal terms with the lawyer).

## Explaining a judgment or order
- Who won what, in one line; the orders made (costs, directions, deadlines) as a list.
- Why the court decided that way, in plain terms.
- What happens next: compliance deadlines, appeal options and **appeal limitation**
  (compute with the legal-deadlines skill), effect on other matters.

## Explaining a contract to a client
Summarise in a one-page table: what you must do, what they must do, money, key dates,
how to get out, what happens if it goes wrong, the three clauses to worry about.

Draft in the reply for short emails; save longer letters to `work/` as .docx or .md.
